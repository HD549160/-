## Context and challenge {#context}

Medication safety is not limited to the moment a pill is taken. Collection, sorting, reminders, confirmation, refills and exception handling are distributed across older adults, family members and medical support. A break in any handoff can increase risk.

> The challenge is not to add another reminder, but to give each participant the right information at the right time.

## Research and insights {#insight}

The portfolio frames the problem through personas, a stakeholder map, current-state ecosystems and a journey map. Key tensions include cognitive and operational load for older adults, limited visibility for families, discontinuity between medical guidance and home routines, and fragmented hardware-software touchpoints.

### Opportunity areas

- Keep the older-adult experience focused on the current task and essential feedback.
- Let the family experience support planning, monitoring and exceptions instead of duplicating the primary interface.
- Use the physical device for routine actions and digital touchpoints for plans, reminders and support relationships.

## Product strategy {#strategy}

The system is organised around three touchpoints: a dispenser and simplified experience for older adults, an application for family management and visibility, and a service relationship with medical support. Priorities centre on taking the correct dose on time, confirming completion and handling exceptions.

No launch data has been supplied, so visual completeness is not treated as proof of effectiveness. The case demonstrates multi-stakeholder organisation, service flow and hardware-software consistency.

## Core solution {#solution}

The device supports storage, dispensing and explicit feedback. The older-adult interface focuses on today’s tasks and simple confirmation. The family interface supports plans, status and remote attention. Together, they cover the path from planning through daily use to exception handling.

## Validation plan {#validation}

Next, low-fidelity prototypes should test whether older adults can complete dispensing, confirmation and recovery tasks independently. Family members should be tested on rapid status interpretation. Multi-day contextual testing should examine reminder fatigue, mistakes and dependency.

Useful measures include task completion, critical errors, assistance requests, status comprehension and perceived workload after repeated use.

## Reflection {#reflection}

The system is broad, but a future version should narrow the initial release and make the hardware-versus-software boundary explicit. Medical claims and safety language also require professional validation rather than inference from a concept presentation.
