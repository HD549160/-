## Context and challenge {#context}

Older family members hold rich personal memories, yet expression styles, digital barriers and mismatched schedules often prevent those memories from being recorded. Younger relatives may want to listen, but interviewing, organising and producing content requires time and skill.

> AI is not the protagonist here; it is a tool for lowering the barrier to recording and expression.

## Research and insights {#insight}

Personas, storyboards and a journey map surface three needs: older adults need a natural way to speak, younger relatives need a lightweight participation path, and shared viewing and response determine whether a record becomes genuine interaction.

Speech-to-animation is presented as a conceptual capability for turning narration into preservable, shared stories. The project does not claim model development or a launched service.

## Product strategy {#strategy}

The core loop is: prompt a theme, narrate, generate a draft, edit together, archive and share. Value is not measured by generation alone, but by whether the experience starts conversations, enriches memories and supports continued interaction.

Priorities are low-friction capture, controllable editing and family participation, without exposing older adults to complex generation settings or content management.

## Core solution {#solution}

Older adults narrate through voice and prompt cards. The system produces a reviewable draft. Family members can add text, images and timeline information. The result enters a personal memory book for shared viewing and response.

## Validation plan {#validation}

First test whether older adults can start, pause and complete a story without continuous assistance. Then assess whether generated drafts preserve meaning and remain easy to correct. Finally, observe whether shared viewing creates follow-up conversation.

Privacy consent, voice and image usage, error correction and family-level visibility require explicit testing.

## Reflection {#reflection}

The concept communicates emotional value, but uncertainty, privacy and content governance need stronger definition. A next version should validate the need for shared recording before deciding whether animation generation is essential.
