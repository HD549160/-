## Context and challenge {#context}

Delivery riders repeatedly check routes, confirm orders, contact customers and complete handoffs while moving. The phone carries essential information, but looking down and switching between tasks adds cognitive load and can affect riding safety.

> The opportunity is to reduce attention switching, not to move the entire phone interface into the rider’s field of view.

## Research and insights {#insight}

The portfolio recreates the journey from accepting an order and collecting food to contacting customers, navigating, confirming arrival and checking income. Frequent, time-sensitive information may suit voice or glanceable display, while complex input and low-frequency management should remain on the phone.

The concept therefore balances information restraint, voice operation, environmental awareness, comfort and structural reliability.

## Product strategy {#strategy}

The helmet handles directions, critical alerts, voice communication and essential safety cues. The phone retains order details, complex input and infrequent management. The first prioritisation question is whether a task must be completed while riding.

Industrial and interaction design constrain each other: display placement affects visibility, audio affects environmental awareness, and additional hardware changes weight, battery life and comfort.

## Core solution {#solution}

The concept integrates an AR display, bone-conduction audio, microphone, lighting and positioning into the helmet structure. First-person scenarios show when navigation and delivery information appears.

## Validation plan {#validation}

Next, simulated riding tasks should test comprehension speed, visual obstruction and voice usability in noise. A physical prototype should evaluate weight distribution, stability, heat, battery life and wet-weather reliability.

Safety-related evaluation must progress through controlled environments; visual polish cannot substitute for real validation.

## Reflection {#reflection}

The current proposal communicates the concept and structure clearly, but includes many functions. A future version should prioritise navigation and critical alerts, prove the core value, and only then expand communication, recording and assistance features.
